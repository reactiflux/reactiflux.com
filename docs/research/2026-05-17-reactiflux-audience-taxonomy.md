## Framing

The segmentation in this document is motivational and relational, not demographic. Two members in radically different geographies can share a motivational shape — *I want calibration with peers I respect on questions I can't ask at work* — and the community's job is the same for both. Conversely, two members in the same city can be hiring Reactiflux for opposite reasons, and a site organized around their nationality would mis-serve both. Demographics appear in the profiles below only where they have material consequence on the questions being asked: a developer earning rupees in Bangalore is in a structurally different position from one earning dollars in Brooklyn, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. Geography matters because it shapes the *jobs the community is being hired for*, not because nationality is itself an identity.

Ten identities, not six and not twenty. Six elides the freelance-climber-vs-established distinction that the activity data and the admin's read of jobs-advice both insist matters. Twenty manufactures vanity completeness — a fake first name and an oat-milk-latte detail for each — and ends up describing none of them well. Ten is the count at which each identity earns a substantive profile without the set losing legibility.

Relative to the seed list: the durable regular, the Indian developer in transition, the React newcomer, the library maintainer, and the long-tail member are kept substantially as-seeded. The West-African and Southeast-Asian freelance-bound identities have been merged into a single **globally distributed freelance climber** — the cross-regional motivational pattern is sharper than the per-region texture, and splitting by region produces three near-identical profiles with different city names. The hinted "established remote freelance professional" is added as a distinct identity (the **established remote independent**) — the climber-to-established arc is the single most consequential career transition in the community's growth audience, and collapsing them hides it. The US/EU mid-career engineer is kept distinct from the durable regular — they are a parallel population and the primary entry path *to* the regulars, not the same people. The cycling jobseeker is *not* a standalone identity: it is a phase that cuts across the climbers, the newcomers, and the mid-career engineers, and naming it separately would double-count; the instrumental visitor identity captures the structurally episodic posture without conflating it with career arc.

## 1. The durable regular

**Sketch.** Thirty to fifty people who have set the tone of the community for roughly eight years. Not a segment among segments — the social infrastructure of the place, the people whose continued presence is what makes Reactiflux Reactiflux. Mostly mid-to-senior engineers, some staff or principal, a handful of library maintainers, a handful of independent consultants. Mostly US, UK, Canada, France, the Netherlands, Germany, with a few in Brazil and India. They know each other's pets. Some have stayed at each other's houses. Usually embarrassed to be described this way.

**What they're hiring Reactiflux for (JTBD).** Continuity of professional company. A small group of peers, encountered casually over years, against whom they calibrate their own taste. Not "belonging" in the marketing sense — closer to the way a regular at a particular bar uses the bar. Not help; they have outgrown that. The texture of overhearing serious people talk seriously about work-adjacent things.

**What they bring.** Almost everything that distinguishes Reactiflux from a chatroom that opened last week. They set the register — sardonic, technically rigorous, generous to honest questioners, impatient with low-effort ones. They write the messages that end threads. They are why the off-topic channels are funny rather than dull. They have memory: they remember when a now-standard library was a controversial idea, and they will tell you about it in three sentences.

**Where they appear on Discord.** #random above all (12,169 messages, 54 chatters in 28 days — the regulars are the spine of that 54). Heavy in private-mvps (14 chatters, 479 messages — the inner-circle channel by the numbers). Active in tooling debates and the senior corners of help-react. Less active in jobs-advice.

**Their relationship to other identities.** They are the implicit longest-arc destination for several other identities — the US/EU mid-career engineer who keeps showing up becomes one over years; the established remote independent occasionally becomes one. They are the audience the newcomer is unconsciously calibrating against. They overlap heavily with the library maintainer and with the cultural-emigré (many regulars are both).

**What the current site fails to do for them.** It does not show that they exist. The site reads, to a regular, as if written for a community they don't recognize — devoid of the textures that make the place actually function. The site does not lie about them; it renders them invisible, which is its own kind of insult.

**What an honest rewrite could offer them.** Not a section — they would find that vain and would be right. Surfaces that, by their existence, communicate that this is a community with memory and voice and taste: TMiR as flagship rather than afterthought, a Learning page opinionated in a way only a particular community could be, a careers register that sounds like the regulars talking. The test is whether they would link to the site from their own bio without wincing.

## 2. The US/EU mid-career engineer using Reactiflux as professional social space

**Sketch.** A working engineer, usually three to twelve years in, often React-adjacent rather than React-primary by 2026, based in the US, UK, Canada, France, Germany, the Netherlands, or Australia. Real job, pays well. Not seeking help; seeking peers. Sometimes their offline professional life has thinned — a remote-first job, a small town, the post-2020 erosion of the conference circuit. Sometimes their workplace simply does not contain anyone they want to talk to at length about taste. Reads more than they post.

**What they're hiring Reactiflux for (JTBD).** Adjacent professional company at a register their workplace cannot supply. Calibration on questions of taste — *is this library actually good or is the hype manufactured; is this framework discourse signal or noise* — that they can't ask their juniors and don't want to ask their seniors. A low-friction social space where they can be technically themselves without performing.

**What they bring.** Quiet legitimacy. Their lurking and occasional posting is the social proof that anchors the channels for everyone else. When they post, they post well — terse, accurate, often funny. They are the bridge between the durable regulars and newer arrivals: a newcomer who sees a senior engineer being warm to a beginner reads that as license to participate.

**Where they appear on Discord.** #random heavily. Tooling and framework debate channels. Selectively in help-react when the question is conceptual. Lightly in off-topic specialty channels (food, fitness, finance). Almost never in jobs-advice except on the giving end.

**Their relationship to other identities.** They are the population the durable regular comes from. A mid-career engineer who keeps the tab open for two years and starts recognizing faces *becomes* a regular — the single most important growth pipeline for the community's load-bearing identity. They overlap with the library maintainer (many maintainers are in this group with one project that crossed a threshold) and with the cultural-emigré. They do not overlap meaningfully with the freelance climber or the Indian developer in transition; they live in different problems.

**What the current site fails to do for them.** It does not signal that this is a community for adults with serious jobs. It reads as a beginner resource, and a mid-career engineer landing on it would have to look hard to find anything that contradicts that read.

**What an honest rewrite could offer them.** Surfaces that confirm what they suspect — senior practitioners with taste, conversation worth their time, off-topic warmth as feature not leak. Visibility into TMiR, the Learning page, and the recurring threads. A way to find the community without first having to forgive the site.

## 3. The Indian developer transitioning toward international remote work

**Sketch.** The largest national cohort in active participation by a wide margin (832 of 4,555 — 18.3%). Disproportionately Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune, Delhi NCR, Chennai, but increasingly tier-2 cities. Most are in the structurally larger tier of Indian developers: tier-2/3 engineering colleges, non-CS converts, polytechnic graduates, self-taught. Many have a TCS/Infosys/Wipro/HCL/Cognizant bench year on the CV. Hindi/English bilingual; English is professional from the first job. Often supporting parents and siblings. Evening hours overlap nicely with US Pacific business hours, which is part of why they are here.

**What they're hiring Reactiflux for (JTBD).** Two distinct jobs, often simultaneously. First: peer presence with developers operating in the international remote market, to absorb the implicit norms — how they talk about scope, how they negotiate, what they take seriously. Second: a venue where the second-step-of-the-career question (how do I get out of the entry-level glut and into direct-remote employment) can be asked without losing face in front of colleagues. The Indian entry-level market is brutal in a way the rest of the brief understates; a junior applies to hundreds of roles, runs the DSA-grind gauntlet, and competes 200-to-500 deep against other juniors.

**What they bring.** Volume, energy, and a particular seriousness about the craft. They are the most LLM-fluent cohort in the community, and conversations about LLM-assisted development are unusually sharp when they are in the room. They are also the cohort with the highest per-capita headroom: if India hit France's per-capita rate, the active Indian membership would be roughly 2,980 — 3.6× the current 832. The growth potential here is not theoretical.

**Where they appear on Discord.** Heavy in help-react (still the second-most-active channel — they are part of why). Active in jobs-advice. Visible in #random. Active in the modern React tooling and Next/Remix/RSC debates, where their production-grade exposure is often stronger than the brief suggests.

**Their relationship to other identities.** Largest overlap with the globally distributed freelance climber — many Indian members are climbers, but the Indian-specific economic dynamic (supply-side glut, Upwork rate-floor) makes the identity distinct enough to name. They share the post-entry mentorship vacuum with the climber. They evolve, when things go well, into the established remote independent, or less often — because of timezone and citizenship friction — into the US/EU mid-career engineer. They are not the same as the React newcomer; many are years past it but still climbing the international-employability ladder.

**What the current site fails to do for them.** Nearly everything that is materially specific to their situation is absent. No content on the India-rates-on-Upwork floor, no peer-visible discussion of the TCS-to-product-company transition, no acknowledgment that this cohort exists as the modal Reactiflux member rather than as an "international users" footnote.

**What an honest rewrite could offer them.** Visibility first — recognition that they are the largest cohort, not a marginal one. Content surfaces that treat second-step career questions as first-class. A careers register that does not assume the reader is in San Francisco. Surfacing the regulars and the established remote independents as proof that the trajectory they are on is real.

## 4. The globally distributed freelance climber

**Sketch.** A developer two to five years into their working life, building a career on Upwork, Toptal, Lemon.io, Arc, Deel, Crossover, and the next tier of regional platforms — *not yet established*, not yet on a stable direct-remote relationship with a foreign employer. Lagos, Karachi, Manila, Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Nairobi, sometimes Cebu or Lahore. The active-data top ten makes this identity concrete: Nigeria (192), Pakistan (161), Philippines (95), Vietnam (88), plus meaningful long-tail presence. English fluent enough to work and negotiate. Self-taught via YouTube, freeCodeCamp, a regional bootcamp (Saylani, ALX Africa, KodeGo, FUNiX), and increasingly LLMs.

**What they're hiring Reactiflux for (JTBD).** Three jobs, in rough order of weight. First: post-entry mentorship — the conversation that takes them from "I landed a $200 Upwork job" to "I have a $50k/year FTE remote contract," which their local resources don't teach because that conversation can only be had with peers who have made the transition. Second: payment, platform, and contract texture — Payoneer vs. Wise, getting paid in dollars from Karachi or Lagos, Deel-mediated employment vs. direct invoicing. Third: peer company in dollar-hours, which often means working nights from a household that does not quite understand the work.

**What they bring.** A particular and underweighted form of seriousness: this work is not lifestyle and not hobby — in purchasing-power terms it is a path into the upper-middle class. They bring questions the community is well-positioned to host. When they stay they become bridges between Reactiflux and the developer scenes of Lagos, Karachi, Manila, and Hanoi.

**Where they appear on Discord.** Jobs-advice heavily (this is where the admin's read of unmet freelance appetite comes from). Active in help-react when stuck. Increasingly visible in #random as they become regulars.

**Their relationship to other identities.** Heavy overlap with the Indian developer in transition — same motivational shape, different national resource picture. They are the population that becomes the established remote independent if the trajectory works. They share questions with the React newcomer when early in the climb, but diverge quickly: the newcomer is hiring the community for technical onboarding, the climber for professional and economic ones. They occasionally evolve into the durable regular over many years, but the more common destination is the established remote independent.

**What the current site fails to do for them.** The site is silent on every load-bearing question of their working life. Nothing about freelance-to-FTE transitions, nothing about the platforms, nothing about payment infrastructure, nothing acknowledging that the global membership has a different center of gravity than the site implies.

**What an honest rewrite could offer them.** A careers surface that treats their questions as central. Curated or community-generated content on the freelance-to-FTE arc. Visibility of the established remote independents as proof the trajectory works. A register that does not assume the reader is in a low-cost-of-living country only as background detail.

## 5. The established remote independent

**Sketch.** Three or more years into a working relationship with foreign clients — either a long-tenured freelancer with stable monthly invoices to two or three Western firms, or a Deel/EOR-mediated FTE for a US or European employer. Based in Lagos, Karachi, Manila, Buenos Aires, Hanoi, Bangalore, Lahore, Nairobi, or Cape Town. Earning meaningfully above their local cost-of-living context. Has solved the early problems the climber is still solving — Payoneer/Wise/Deel/Stripe, contract scope, client relationships, the social/family adjustment.

**What they're hiring Reactiflux for (JTBD).** Peer company at their actual register, which is hard to find locally. They are typically more experienced than most of the local meetup population, more senior than the climber cohort they came from, less tied to a traditional employer's social orbit than the US/EU mid-career engineer. The next step is not "land a first foreign client" but "should I take the principal-level offer that requires relocation, or stay independent with three stable contracts and double the autonomy" — a question with almost no good public forum.

**What they bring.** Mentorship at exactly the rung where the community most needs it. They are the proof-of-trajectory for the climber and the Indian developer in transition. They have war stories that are not on YouTube. When they post in jobs-advice, the climbers read carefully, because this is what next looks like.

**Where they appear on Discord.** Active in jobs-advice (often on the answering side now). Visible in #random. Selectively in help-react when conceptual. Some are in private-mvps. Not loud, but weighty.

**Their relationship to other identities.** Destination of the climber and partial destination of the Indian developer in transition. Overlaps with the library maintainer (a meaningful number of maintainers are independent professionals based outside the US/EU). Shares the senior register with the US/EU mid-career engineer. Over a long enough timeline, can become a durable regular.

**What the current site fails to do for them.** The site does not acknowledge that this trajectory exists, let alone that it is well-represented. There is no surface where the question *"I am earning a US salary from Lagos / Karachi / Buenos Aires, what should I think about next"* is hosted.

**What an honest rewrite could offer them.** Implicit recognition that they are real and present, through content surfaces and a careers register that takes their stage of the trajectory seriously. The opportunity to be visibly useful to the climbers behind them — one of the things this identity hires the community for.

## 6. The React newcomer

**Sketch.** Three months to two years into learning React, sometimes longer. Any geography, but with a heavy lean toward populations where local mentorship is thinnest — early climbers, junior developers from India, Brazil, and the long tail, plus the genuinely entry-level US/UK/Canadian self-taught person. Has done some combination of YouTube (Apna College, CodeWithHarry, Rocketseat, Web Dev Simplified), freeCodeCamp, a bootcamp, and a meaningful amount of LLM-assisted learning. Has a working app or two. Unsure how much of what they know is real understanding versus pattern-matching against Claude's autocomplete.

**What they're hiring Reactiflux for (JTBD).** Human review and human company. Crucially: not technical answers — those they can get from an LLM faster — but the social validation that comes from a working developer telling them their code is fine, or telling them why it isn't. They are hiring the community to confirm that they exist as a developer, which is a job no LLM can do. They also lurk to absorb the implicit register of professional discourse.

**What they bring.** Volume in help-react (still 1,299 messages and 78 chatters in 28 days — evidence that human-mediated React help is alive after the LLM wave). The energy of people newly in love with the craft. They will, in time, become climbers, mid-career engineers, or established independents — they are upstream of most of the other growth identities.

**Where they appear on Discord.** Help-react above all. Increasingly in help-typescript and the tooling channels as they grow. Lightly in #random as they gain confidence. Sometimes in jobs-advice when seeking direction.

**Their relationship to other identities.** Upstream of nearly every other identity. A newcomer who stays often becomes a climber (if in Lagos, Karachi, Manila, Hanoi), the Indian developer in transition (if in Bangalore, Pune, Hyderabad), or the mid-career engineer over many years (if in the US/EU and a job materializes). Downstream of no other identity — this is the genuine entry point.

**What the current site fails to do for them.** It does not host expectations. A newcomer landing on the site cannot tell whether to expect "ask any question, get a patient answer," "the channel will tell you to read the docs first," or "you should be asking your LLM first" — all of which are sometimes true.

**What an honest rewrite could offer them.** Honest framing of what the community is and is not for a newcomer in 2026 — not a learn-React landing page (a soft lie), but a clear sense that this is a place where humans review the work the LLM helped them write, and where the conversation is at a register worth aspiring to. A Learning page that is opinionated. An invitation that is real.

## 7. The library maintainer or topical expert passing through

**Sketch.** Small in number, outsized in influence. Mark Erikson is the archetype; perhaps fifteen to forty others active across any given six-month window. Authors and primary maintainers of widely-used packages in the React ecosystem, plus a smaller group of topical experts (state management, build tooling, types, accessibility, performance) recognized even without a single flagship project. Mostly US/EU based, with a growing minority from the global membership. Sometimes employed by a company that pays them to do open-source work, sometimes independent.

**What they're hiring Reactiflux for (JTBD).** A venue. A place to be visibly useful in a way lower-stakes than GitHub issues and higher-leverage than personal Twitter/X. The community is also a research instrument — what people are actually asking, what they are confused by — that informs their own design and documentation work. And peer company with other maintainers, which is uniquely scarce.

**What they bring.** A disproportionate share of the community's authority and content. The Learning page Mark curates is more or less this identity's contribution rendered as a site surface. TMiR draws heavily on this population's signal. Their occasional presence in help-react changes the character of the channel — a newcomer watching a maintainer explain why their library does what it does is learning something they could not have learned alone.

**Where they appear on Discord.** Selectively. The relevant help-react thread, occasionally their library's own channel if it exists, sometimes #random, sometimes private-mvps. Not high-volume, but high-weight.

**Their relationship to other identities.** Heavy overlap with the durable regular — many are durable regulars by another name. Shared register with the US/EU mid-career engineer and the established remote independent. They are the implicit aspirational figure for the React newcomer (often invisibly) and a recognized one for the Indian developer in transition.

**What the current site fails to do for them.** It does not amplify their contribution. The Learning page exists but is buried. TMiR exists but is not flagship. The site does not make the community legibly a place where serious practitioners spend their time, which is the strongest evidence in favor of being one.

**What an honest rewrite could offer them.** Surfaces that take their contribution seriously and make it durable. A Learning page that is a flagship. TMiR with proper site-level presence. An aesthetic of citation — when a maintainer's post answers a recurring question, the answer becoming part of the site's memory.

## 8. The globally distributed tail member

**Sketch.** One of the roughly 1,997 active members in the 28-day data who live in countries outside the top ten — a population 2.4× larger than the largest single national cohort. Ukraine, Argentina, Kenya, Egypt, Thailand, Syria-diaspora, Indonesia, Turkey, Germany, the Netherlands, Australia, South Africa, Colombia, Mexico, Romania, Poland, Bangladesh, the long list. Often the only React developer in their immediate social orbit. English fluent enough to participate. Career stage varies widely.

**What they're hiring Reactiflux for (JTBD).** Professional society. The community is, for them, the React community in their language and timezone — there isn't another one. They may be the only React developer in their company, their city, sometimes their country at the register Reactiflux operates. They are hiring the community to render their work non-solitary.

**What they bring.** Geographic and temporal coverage — they are why someone posting in help-react at 3am Pacific gets an answer within minutes. They bring the actual texture of the "global" in "globally distributed" and perspectives the top-ten countries do not have (Argentinian fintech, Indonesian super-app, Egyptian Arabic-speaking dev tooling). Often the population most likely to bring back unusual problems from contexts the dominant React discourse has not addressed.

**Where they appear on Discord.** Across every channel, lightly per person, heavily in aggregate. They are part of the reason #random sustains 24-hour activity. They make help-react a venue rather than a US-business-hours queue.

**Their relationship to other identities.** Cross-cuts every other identity. Some are climbers, some established independents, some newcomers, some mid-career engineers in unusual locations. The unifying motivational shape is geographic isolation. They share many traits with the Indian developer in transition and with the climber, but live in a national long tail that lacks any single big-cohort dynamic.

**What the current site fails to do for them.** Does not signal that they exist. The site implicitly addresses an American or maybe European audience. It does not make the global-distribution fact of the community visible.

**What an honest rewrite could offer them.** A site that reads as written by and for a globally distributed community rather than an American one with global users. Visible acknowledgment of the long tail. Content surfaces that do not assume a US/EU baseline.

## 9. The cultural-emigré social-hangout member

**Sketch.** A subset of the community for whom Reactiflux is not primarily a professional resource but a social space. Often remote workers in small towns, parents of young children, recent immigrants navigating loneliness, people whose university friends scattered. They post in off-topic channels — gaming, movies, food, finance, fitness — more than in technical ones, and the activity data is unambiguous that these channels collectively rival technical activity in aggregate. They remember other members' birthdays.

**What they're hiring Reactiflux for (JTBD).** Social warmth at the register of a profession they share — not the same as a random Discord, which would lack the implicit common ground. They are hiring the community to provide the low-friction professional adjacency that a co-located office or university campus used to provide automatically.

**What they bring.** The social texture that retains the regulars. Without them the community reverts to a Q&A site with worse search, and the durable regulars wander off. Often the people who keep the off-topic culture warm and not weird.

**Where they appear on Discord.** Off-topic channels — gaming, movies, food, finance, fitness, music. Active in #random. Lighter in technical channels.

**Their relationship to other identities.** Significant overlap with the durable regular (many regulars are also cultural emigrés, and the two identities can be the same person at different moments). Substantial overlap with the US/EU mid-career engineer. Some overlap with the established remote independent, whose working life is structurally isolated. Less overlap with the climber or newcomer, who are hiring the community for more instrumental reasons in their first years.

**What the current site fails to do for them.** It treats the social character of the community as either invisible or as a leak. The off-topic richness is one of the strongest things Reactiflux has, and the site does not make it legible.

**What an honest rewrite could offer them.** Surfaces that acknowledge the social warmth as a feature. Implicit recognition that the off-topic channels are a meaningful part of what the community is. A register that does not pretend the community is purely technical.

## 10. The visitor with an instrumental question

**Sketch.** Lands on the site or in Discord with a specific question — usually job- or career-flavored, sometimes technical. Found Reactiflux through search or word of mouth. Does not intend to become a regular, and probably won't. Asks the question, gets an answer or doesn't, leaves. Some return periodically — every couple of years at a career transition — and treat the community as a kind of career-reagent. The cycling jobseeker is the dominant subtype; the identity is broader than that — anyone whose relationship with the community is structurally episodic and instrumental.

**What they're hiring Reactiflux for (JTBD).** A specific answer. Honest framing of what kind of help is available. They are hiring the community for a transaction, not a relationship, and this is fine — a community that cannot host instrumental visitors has confused itself with a club.

**What they bring.** Search traffic, link-back, occasional content the community itself reuses. A small fraction become regulars after several visits; most don't, and shouldn't be expected to. They are a steady source of the recurring instrumental questions that the durable regulars and established independents enjoy answering — *what should I be paid, is this offer competitive, how do I get my first international client* — which keep the relevant channels alive.

**Where they appear on Discord.** Jobs-advice heavily. Help-react when they have a one-off question. Looking-for-group. Rarely in #random or off-topic.

**Their relationship to other identities.** Shares questions with the climber, the Indian developer in transition, the established remote independent, and the newcomer, depending on what they came for — but their *relationship* with the community is different, because they are not in a posture of building one. A visitor whose return visits stack across years can quietly become any of those identities, sometimes a durable regular over a long enough timeline, but this is rare and should not be planned for.

**What the current site fails to do for them.** Does not make the available registers of help legible from the outside. A visitor cannot tell whether to expect the warmth the community actually offers or the cold transactional Q&A the site's tone implies.

**What an honest rewrite could offer them.** Search-findable, honestly framed answers to recurring instrumental questions — careers, jobs, the freelance-to-FTE arc, what kind of help to expect — surfaced as community content rather than marketing. A landing experience that is honest about being a community rather than a service, and useful even to a visitor who is not going to stay.

## Relationship map

The taxonomy resolves into a small number of trajectories and a larger set of co-occupied surfaces. The structure agent should treat the map below as the explicit contract.

**Primary growth trajectory (entry → established).** The React newcomer is the genuine entry point for the long-arc identities. From the newcomer, the trajectory splits by geography and economic context. A newcomer in Lagos, Karachi, Manila, or Hanoi typically becomes a globally distributed freelance climber within a year or two. A newcomer in Bangalore, Pune, or Hyderabad becomes the Indian developer in transition — a related but structurally distinct shape, because of the scale of the Indian entry-level glut and the brutally low Upwork rate-floor for Indian developers specifically. A newcomer in the US, UK, Canada, or France with a job becomes, over years, the US/EU mid-career engineer. The climber and the Indian developer in transition both feed forward into the established remote independent if the trajectory works. The mid-career engineer and the established remote independent both feed forward, over many years, into the durable regular — the longest-arc destination and the load-bearing identity for the community as a whole.

**Parallel pipelines.** The library maintainer is its own pipeline, mostly emerging from the mid-career engineer population but increasingly from the established remote independent population as well. The cultural-emigré social-hangout identity is not a destination — it is a posture that can attach to several other identities, most often the durable regular and the mid-career engineer.

**Cross-cuts.** The globally distributed tail member is a cross-cut, not a stage: a tail member in Cairo can be at any career stage, and the unifying job is geographic isolation. The visitor with an instrumental question is also a cross-cut — a posture that briefly attaches to a person on some other trajectory or none.

**Co-occupied surfaces.** Help-react is shared by the newcomer, the climber, the Indian developer in transition, the maintainer, and the mid-career engineer — five identities, four registers, one channel. Jobs-advice is shared by the climber, the Indian developer in transition, the established remote independent, the visitor, and (on the answering side) the mid-career engineer and the durable regular. #random is shared by the durable regular, the mid-career engineer, the cultural-emigré, the established remote independent, increasingly the climber, and a substantial fraction of the tail. TMiR and the Learning page are flagships of the maintainer and the durable regular, read by everyone else. Private-mvps is the durable regular and a small group of maintainers and established independents.

**What this means for site architecture.** The site should not address identities sequentially as if they were market segments. It should host surfaces — careers, learning, TMiR, the community texture itself — at registers the load-bearing identities recognize as theirs, with on-ramps that let the earlier-stage identities see where the trajectory leads. The newcomer should be able to see, from the careers surface, that the climber and the established remote independent are real. The climber should be able to see that the established remote independent is real. The mid-career engineer should be able to see that the durable regular is real. The site's job is to make the community's trajectories legible from the outside — which is the same as showing the regulars, the maintainers, and the established independents in their actual presence, rather than describing the audience to itself.
